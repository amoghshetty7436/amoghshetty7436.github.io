Tracking every bag on the belt

Airlines mishandled about 26 million bags in 2022. The founder, an aeronautical engineer, wanted to catch a misrouted bag on the airport's existing CCTV before it ever left the building. I was one of two students picked from campus to build the vision system.

No airport data, so we built it

We didn't have airport footage yet. I scraped bag images from the web, hand-picked them, and annotated every one in the VGG Image Annotator into two classes: tag attached, and tag missing. Tags fall off when bags collide or the belt runs fast, and an untagged bag is a lost bag.

A classifier was accurate but too slow for live video, so I moved to a YOLOv8 detector, which handled both classes in real time. I built a second dataset the same way for damage such as broken wheels and dents.

The routing idea. When staff scan the tag at check-in, the bag's ID and destination are linked. If a Chennai bag drifts onto the wrong belt, the system alerts staff.

From one camera to many

Inside one camera, YOLOv8 plus ByteTrack gave each bag a stable ID. The hard part was keeping that ID when a bag leaves one camera and enters the next.

No public video shows the same bag passing through different cameras, so we had nothing to validate against. We swapped bags for people, where multi-camera datasets exist (Market-1501). TransReID turned each person into an embedding, and cosine similarity matched them across views.

Then I tested it for real: I filmed a friend in the dark, from above, far away and inside crowds. One video was used to enroll him and the rest for testing. In every one the system gave him the same ID, 1.

What happened next

The founder pitched the system to airports, who liked the results, and we secured a meeting with an airport official on the promise of replicating it for bags once we had their footage. I then left for my IIT Bombay research internship. I'm still in touch with the founder.

It taught me what ML engineering looks like in practice. You often build the dataset before you build the model, and when the data you need doesn't exist, you change the problem until you can test it honestly.
