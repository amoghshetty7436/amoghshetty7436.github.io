Listening for forest elephants

Forest elephants live in rainforest too dense to survey by sight, so the Elephant Listening Project records them. Sensor grids across Central and West Africa capture 24 hours of audio a day. My job is to make the models that find elephants, and the gunshots that threaten them, in all that sound.

Why sound

Elephants talk in rumbles, mostly below what people can hear, around 10 to 30 Hz with harmonics stacked above. Those calls travel far through the forest. Gunshots from poachers are short and broadband. Both show up clearly on a spectrogram, but nobody can listen to years of recordings by hand.

In progress. This project started in October 2026. I'll add results here as they're ready.

What I'm working on

  • Benchmarking open-source rumble detectors and bioacoustic foundation models such as AVES on 24-hour recordings from sensor grids.
  • Building a training and evaluation pipeline that scales to terabytes of audio, aiming for better accuracy and faster inference.
  • Designing a new rumble detection model, and a gunshot detector for anti-poaching monitoring.
